# Evidence and standards for an autonomous RL research harness

**Recommendation:** build the system around falsifiable experiment plans, independently computed evaluation, and durable evidence records. Use agents to propose and challenge research decisions; use executable checks and fresh experiments to decide whether their claims survive. A successful run, unanimous council, or polished manuscript is not by itself scientific validation.

**Review date:** 2026-11-01, America/Los_Angeles. **Status:** design guidance, not an implemented or evaluated system. **Scope:** conventional RL and LLM post-training, including tool-using agents, through separate adapters. This document separates published observations from proposed engineering requirements.

## Scope and evidence quality

The search covered RL empirical methodology, automated reward/environment design, autonomous ML research, self-correction/debate, and LLM verifier robustness, including explicit searches for 2026 and October 2026 work. The selected evidence reaches an October 14, 2026 preprint; this is a selective review, not an exhaustive statement of the latest frontier. Dates below come from proceedings or arXiv version histories, rather than search-engine crawl dates. Some OpenReview pages presented browser challenges; official ICLR proceedings and author arXiv versions supplied accessible alternatives. The Nature article below was inspected through its full-text PMC copy after the publisher redirected to an inaccessible identity endpoint.

Conference papers, preprints, and conference policy have different evidential roles. Accepted papers support particular methods and findings under their experimental conditions. Preprints identify promising mechanisms to test. The conference handbook describes submission expectations; it does not certify research quality. No source here establishes that combining these mechanisms yields reliable, general, unattended scientific discovery.

These papers motivate an LLM-specific validation layer; they do not justify replacing conventional RL validation with language-model evaluation. Both adapters should share run identity, provenance, budgets, and lifecycle events while exposing distinct trajectory and metric schemas.

## Proposed research workflow

The following is a design recommendation informed by the sources above, not an experimentally validated combined method.

1. **Define the question and the claim it could support.** Save the intended contribution, competing explanations, target populations or tasks, expected failure modes, and the smallest experiment capable of distinguishing alternatives. “Improve reward” is underspecified until success is defined independently of that reward.
2. **Build a cited evidence packet.** Retrieve close prior work and opposing evidence, record exact versions and sections, and separate author claims from reproduced findings. Explain the method difference that could make the experiment informative. A paper title or abstract alone cannot support a detailed implementation decision.
3. **Review the proposed experiment.** Agents independently assess scientific value, RL validity, statistical design, and feasibility before reading one another's conclusions. The orchestrator records actionable objections and either revises the plan or requests a discriminating pilot.
4. **Freeze a versioned experiment manifest.** Bind hypothesis, code/environment/reward hashes, data provenance, comparison conditions, seeds, tuning budget, metrics, evaluation split, compute ceiling, and stopping/selection rules. Changes after seeing results create a new exploratory version; they do not silently rewrite the original comparison.
5. **Run contract checks and a pilot.** Validate interfaces and semantics, then measure runtime, numerical behavior, and whether the proposed comparison produces observable differences. A passing smoke test only licenses the next stage, not the scientific claim.
6. **Execute within the manifest's budget.** Record operational events and complete run outcomes, including failures. Preserve enough information to distinguish an interrupted run from a negative result. Resume the same run only when checkpoint semantics are valid; otherwise create a linked new run.
7. **Evaluate and challenge.** Compute metrics from preserved artifacts with evaluation code outside the experiment author's write permissions. Compare against baselines, quantify uncertainty, and attempt to falsify the leading interpretation. Reserve new evaluation tasks or seeds for confirmation after exploratory selection.
8. **Decide and consolidate.** Choose continue, replicate, revise, abandon, or escalate. Record the evidence, unresolved objections, and why the next experiment is worth its cost. Update a concise project state file while retaining immutable underlying artifacts and earlier decisions.

## RL environment validity is a separate deliverable

For every new environment, require an **environment specification** covering observations and hidden state, action constraints, transition dynamics, randomness, reward terms, episode termination versus external time limits, reset distribution, and evaluation distribution. Explain how these choices represent the actual research question. An interface-valid environment can still measure the wrong behavior.

Validation should include deterministic fixtures where appropriate, seeded reset checks, observation/action bounds, finite numerical outputs, termination cases, and trivial policies such as random, no-op, and simple heuristics. Domain-specific invariants matter more than a generic checklist: for a resource allocation task, conservation and budget accounting may expose invalid shortcuts; for navigation, reachability and information leakage may matter more.

Keep the training reward distinct from the success evaluator. Inspect reward-component trajectories, unsuccessful high-reward behaviors, repeated or boundary actions, and held-out task variants. A curriculum must demonstrate both learning progress and transfer; success only on generated training tasks does not establish broader improvement. These are proposed acceptance checks, not claims that any cited paper supplies a universal test suite.

## LLM and tool-using agent adapter requirements

The following interface requirements are engineering recommendations. A trainer adapter should record the base checkpoint, training stage and parent stage, tokenizer/chat template, prompt/task IDs, generation settings, response-length limits, reward/verifier versions, and task-level rollout grouping. For tool-using agents, preserve tool schemas, observed responses, environment reset conditions, allowed actions, and termination causes. Record policy versions when rollouts may be generated asynchronously, so stale trajectories can be identified rather than silently pooled.

Expose reward components separately from the total scalar and retain verifier outcomes as typed records. Distinguish task failure from an invalid output, parse error, timeout, and verifier/infrastructure exception. The experiment policy may explicitly map some statuses to a penalty or retry, but the raw status must remain available. Otherwise the model can be trained against service reliability rather than the intended task.

Freeze the evaluation inference protocol as carefully as the training configuration: temperature and sampling settings, maximum tokens, tool/call/time budget, sample count, and whether the metric is pass@1, pass@k, majority-selected, or verifier-selected. These metrics answer different questions. Retain per-task outputs and aggregate uncertainty at the appropriate level; multiple completions of one prompt do not become independent training runs or distinct tasks. If comparing whole systems rather than fixed-compute models, report the resulting performance/cost tradeoff explicitly.

Test the evaluator with known-correct, known-incorrect, malformed, empty, and adversarially constructed outputs. Add task-preserving transformations only when their invariance can be justified; for instance, a rule intended to generalize across object identities should survive appropriate renaming. Keep substantive task quality distinct from format compliance and include hidden cases the reward-authoring worker cannot inspect or alter. For code or tool environments, execution success and passing visible tests do not alone establish the requested outcome.

During training, monitor length and truncation rates, reward distributions and their components, verifier status rates, and divergence between reward and independent success. For group-relative methods, log the fraction of groups with no reward variation, group membership, and the configured normalization/clipping behavior. Interpret these signals against the actual implementation; a uniform-reward group is a diagnostic observation, not a universal reason to terminate a run.

## Training monitoring must distinguish failure from weak evidence

Use inexpensive deterministic monitors for liveness, exit status, stale metrics, NaNs/infinities, resource exhaustion, checkpoint progress, and budget consumption. Dispatch an analysis agent only when a meaningful event requires interpretation. Constant LLM narration of every metric update adds cost without adding measurements.

Training adapters should expose common outcomes (run identity, time/steps, objective metrics, resource usage, checkpoint references) plus algorithm-specific diagnostics. PPO may expose entropy, KL, clipping, and value statistics; another trainer may need different signals. “Watch any model” therefore means a stable monitoring contract with adapters, not one universal convergence rule.

Keep operational stopping and statistical stopping separate. A non-finite loss or exhausted budget can trigger a deterministic stop. Terminating a run because its noisy curve looks weak changes the selection process and can invalidate a naive final comparison. Scientific early stopping, checkpoint selection, and hyperparameter search require declared rules and appropriate evaluation. Do not disguise failed or pruned seeds as absent observations.

## Council protocol and complexity routing

The council's job is to expose missing evidence and propose decisive checks. It should not reward rhetorical victory or agreement. Different role labels do not create independent expertise, and different models can still share errors.

| Stage | Required output | Reason for the stage |
|---|---|---|
| Independent review | Recommendation, supporting source/artifact IDs, most consequential objection, uncertainty, proposed test | Reduces immediate anchoring on the first answer and exposes distinct assumptions. |
| Objection exchange | Each objection labeled resolved, unresolved, or invalid, with evidence | Converts criticism into work that could change the decision. |
| Revision | Explicit changes to plan and the observations that caused them | Prevents superficial paraphrase from counting as correction. |
| Orchestrator decision | Chosen action, rejected alternatives, residual dissent, budget, next falsification condition | Makes the result auditable and executable. |

Start with at most two critique/revision rounds for substantial decisions; continue only if a new source, test, or unresolved material issue justifies another round. This is a proposed cost-control policy, not an optimum established by the literature. If evidence is missing, run a discriminating experiment instead of asking more agents to speculate. Preserve dissent even when the final action proceeds.

Use deterministic programs for numerical aggregation and checks. Assign bounded source extraction, log classification, and manifest linting to cheaper workers when their outputs can be verified. Use stronger models for unfamiliar methodological choices, cross-paper synthesis, ambiguous failures, and final scientific claims. Escalate on contradictory evidence, repeated repair failure, or decisions with substantial compute consequences. Select actual model tiers by measured task performance and cost; job titles alone do not justify a particular model.

Save concise decision rationales: alternatives, evidence, objections, revisions, and remaining uncertainty. These records support reproducibility. They should not be presented as a faithful transcript of a model's inaccessible internal reasoning.

## Literature and memory that resist drift

Maintain a source ledger with canonical URL/DOI/arXiv ID, version date, verified venue status, retrieval date, section/page, claim supported, limitation, and linked project decision. Distinguish “identifier exists” from “source supports this claim.” Preserve the source text or a permitted local reference so later agents can check the interpretation.

A literature watcher should search around the project's hypotheses, methods, datasets, and named competing approaches. Rank new work by its potential to change a decision, including negative results and contradictory findings. On finding a relevant paper, produce an impact note: what changes, why the methods are comparable, whether replication is needed, and which experiment manifest would be revised. Do not silently update an active experiment because a newer paper appeared.

Use Markdown for human-readable project intent, evidence summaries, decision records, current state, and handoffs. Treat these as views over a structured experiment ledger, not as the sole source of truth. Every result summary should link to run IDs and artifacts; every durable design claim should link to evidence or be marked as an assumption. Older claims should be superseded explicitly, never overwritten in a way that hides their origin.

## What would demonstrate that the harness is good

Evaluate the harness before granting it broader research autonomy. A useful initial test set contains a baseline reproduction, a planted environment bug, a deceptive reward shortcut, a failed training run that can be repaired, a null result that should not be oversold, and a literature update that should change a design decision. Ground truth for each should come from fixtures, expert review, or independently reproduced outcomes.

Compare a single capable agent against independent reviewers and a full council under matched total cost and time. Measure valid experiment completion, unsupported claim rate, correct bug detection, reproduction success, appropriate abandonment, useful next-experiment choices, and budget adherence. Count recovery from incorrect criticism as well as successful corrections. A council that writes longer reports without improving these outcomes should be simplified.

The scientific core remains to be built even if a repository supplies excellent agent loops, sessions, background execution, or self-modification. Those are runtime capabilities. Calling them a ready research assistant would require evidence of environment validity, defensible experimental comparisons, faithful reporting, and reliable operation on the intended projects. Likewise, “top conference quality” should remain an evaluation objective expressed through concrete contributions and evidence, not an agent-assigned status.
